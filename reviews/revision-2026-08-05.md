# Revision log — `main_asus_en.tex`, 5 Aug 2026

Reframed from an IC-RCE contribution paper to a scheduling-policy measurement paper, and
re-based every number on `benchmark/05-08-26.md`.

## What changed

**Title.** "SyncPilot: A Lightweight Initial-Calibration Scheduler…" →
"Static Core Pinning Outperforms Linux CFS/EAS Under Contention on Asymmetric Multicores"
(subtitle trimmed in round 2).

**Thesis.** The claim is no longer "IC-RCE is a novel scheduling mechanism". It is:
*explicit static Performance/Efficient pinning loses to CFS/EAS on an idle machine and beats
it at every contention level we measured.* SyncPilot is now framed as the measurement vehicle,
not the contribution.

**IC-RCE demoted to an ablation, with a negative result.** The new data has the
`D-NOTP` scenarios (`SYNCPILOT_DISABLE_TWOPOOL=1`: pinning on, cost-gating off), which is the
ablation the earlier review demanded. Cost-gated routing is slower than plain pinning at all
seven contention levels (3.6–8.3%, six of seven at *p* < 0.01). The paper now says so, and
explains it as a capacity-blind load partition (see round 2 — the work-conservation
explanation drafted here was wrong and was replaced).

**New data axis.** Added the multi-tenant family (2–5 co-running FSRCNN applications,
4 workers each, unpinned) alongside the synthetic-noise family. Seven contention levels × three
policies is now the paper's core table (Table III, `table*`).

**Statistics.** Every cell now carries a standard deviation, and all headline gaps are
backed by Welch's unequal-variance *t*-test (n = 7 steady-state runs). Verified in this session:
static-vs-CFS gaps all *p* < 10⁻⁴; the 10W idle comparison is *p* = 0.68 and is stated as
not significant.

**Figures.** Both regenerated from the new data.
- `fsrcnn_contention_asus.png` (new, `figure*`) — the crossover, two panels, error bars.
- `fsrcnn_throughput_asus.png` (replaced) — idle scaling; the previous file was stale
  (showed 134.89 FPS, matching neither benchmark session).

**Sections removed:** "Calibration Overhead Analysis", "Why Additional Workers Scale",
"Race Condition Mitigation" (folded into correctness), "CFS Baseline Comparison" (superseded).
**Sections added:** "The Idle Operating Point Favours CFS/EAS", "Behaviour Under Contention",
"Ablation: Cost-Gated Stage Routing", "When Static Pinning Is the Wrong Choice".

5. **Deadlock disebut** — §III-C. Versi awal gating menegakkan preferensi dua arah secara ketat; pada konfigurasi homogen (8W, semua Performance) stage ringan tidak bisa diambil siapa pun dan pipeline stall. Diperbaiki dengan fallback work-conserving penuh + guard single-class. Dibingkai sebagai temuan desain: class-preference routing adalah liveness hazard tanpa escape work-conserving, bukan sekadar heuristik yang degrade gracefully.
